# Experiment 001: Event-led LinkedIn content

**Originally tested:** August 2026  
**Documented:** September 2026  
**Channel:** LinkedIn Organic  
**Area:** B2B Content & Social

## Context

I was managing organic LinkedIn content for a B2B SaaS brand and noticed a significant difference in performance during a week where our content was heavily focused on an industry event.

Rather than relying on designed brand creative, the content featured real event photography, people from our team, an integration partner and speakers involved in the event.

## Question

Do event-led LinkedIn posts published while an industry event is happening outperform typical organic brand content, and what characteristics might be driving that performance?

## Hypothesis

Event-led content could outperform typical branded content for a few reasons:

1. **Cross-pollination through tagging.** Tagging speakers, partners and team members gives the content opportunities to travel beyond the brand's existing audience and into relevant professional networks.

2. **Industry association builds trust.** In B2B, audiences pay attention to who companies work with and where they show up. Being visibly associated with complementary partners and recognised industry players can strengthen credibility around the company and its products.

3. **Real people make the brand more human.** Event photography puts actual people behind the company rather than relying solely on polished branded creative, making the content feel more immediate and authentic.

4. **Immediacy matters.** Content published while an event is actually happening may be more relevant and interesting than simply announcing attendance in advance.


## What I tested

I reviewed three LinkedIn posts published on the day of B2B industry events between July and August 2026 and compared their performance with a sample of seven non-event organic posts published across July to September.

The comparison sample included a mix of customer stories, newsletter content, seasonal creative and an event announcement rather than selecting only the strongest-performing posts.

I compared:

- Impressions
- Click-through rate (CTR)
- Engagement rate (ER)

I kept a high-performing educational carousel out of this comparison because it represents a different content format and hypothesis that I want to analyse separately.
