# Experiment 002: Actionable LinkedIn carousels

**Channel:** LinkedIn Organic  
**Area:** B2B Content & Social

## Question

Do actionable, educational LinkedIn carousels drive stronger engagement than typical organic brand content, and what makes some carousel topics perform significantly better than others?

## Hypothesis

Actionable carousel content could outperform typical organic content for a few reasons:

1. **The format encourages deeper engagement.** Unlike a single-image or text post, a carousel asks the audience to actively swipe through multiple slides, giving them more opportunities to engage with the content.

2. **It delivers value within the feed.** Checklists and practical frameworks give the audience useful information without requiring them to leave LinkedIn or download something first.

3. **Topic relevance may matter as much as format.** The same carousel format can produce very different results depending on how timely and broadly relevant the subject is to the audience.

The difference between the first two posts raises another question.

The peak season checklist was published as retailers were beginning to prepare for peak, making the topic particularly timely for an ecommerce audience. By comparison, HVNL compliance is a more specialised subject and the terminology may be less immediately familiar to a broader LinkedIn audience.

This suggests that the format alone may not explain performance. The combination of **format + utility + audience relevance + timing** may be more important.

## What I tested

I reviewed three educational LinkedIn carousels published between July and September 2026.

Each post was designed to deliver practical information directly within the LinkedIn feed, but covered a different B2B topic:

- **HVNL readiness checklist:** A compliance-focused checklist prompting fleet operators to assess whether they could demonstrate operational compliance.
- **Peak season delivery checklist:** Four practical questions for retailers preparing their delivery operations for peak season.
- **Dynamic optimisation:** Incremental steps for moving from static or zone-based routing towards dynamic optimisation.

I compared:

- Impressions
- Clicks
- Click-through rate (CTR)
- Engagement rate (ER)

I also compared the pattern against the typical non-event organic content reviewed in Experiment 001, where engagement rates generally sat around 3–5%.

## Results

All three actionable carousels generated stronger engagement than the typical organic content previously reviewed, although the magnitude varied significantly.

| Carousel | Impressions | Clicks | CTR | Engagement rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HVNL readiness checklist | 1,350 | 96 | 7.11% | 9.70% |
| Peak season delivery checklist | 626 | 184 | 29.39% | 32.43% |
| Dynamic optimisation | 386 | 186 | 48.20% | 50.26% |
| **Average** | **787** | **155** | **28.23%** | **30.80%** |
| **Median** | **626** | **184** | **29.39%** | **32.43%** |

The strongest result came from the dynamic optimisation carousel, which generated a **48.20% CTR and 50.26% engagement rate**.

Interestingly, it also had the **lowest reach of the three posts**, with 386 impressions.

This suggests an important distinction: the carousel format did not necessarily increase distribution. Instead, the strongest posts generated unusually deep engagement among the people who did see them.

### Format alone doesn't explain the result

The three posts used a similar educational carousel format but produced very different levels of engagement.

The HVNL checklist generated a 9.70% engagement rate, while the peak season and dynamic optimisation posts reached 32.43% and 50.26% respectively.

That makes it unlikely that "carousel = higher engagement" is the whole explanation.

Topic relevance, timing, specificity, audience fit and the usefulness of the information may all influence how strongly the format performs.

### Important caveat

This is a small observational sample rather than a controlled test. Differences in topic, timing, creative, copy and audience distribution mean the results cannot isolate the carousel format as the cause of higher engagement.

The pattern is strong enough to justify further testing, but not to conclude that the format alone produced the uplift.
